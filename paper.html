Abstract

We present a conceptual framework — the Resonant Hierarchy — in which all levels of physical organisation from subatomic vibrations to the large-scale structure of the universe are described by a single recursive formula Pn+1 = F(Pn, Cn, En), where P, C, E denote objects, connections, and emergent properties at each level. The emergent properties at level n are derived from the connections of the level below via En = ∂Cn−1/∂Pn. From this structure, several results follow without free parameters: the minimum number of quarks in a proton (three, from ∑D = 0 in three dimensions), the Pauli exclusion principle (from phase antisymmetry), and the phase transitions of water (from T · ΔS = ΔH). Additionally, we report numerical observations relating particle masses and coupling constants with 94–99% accuracy that require independent verification. The framework is conceptual; rigorous Lagrangian formalisation remains an open problem.

§ 1

Introduction

Modern physics describes nature through separate theories at separate scales: quantum mechanics governs the very small, general relativity governs the very large, thermodynamics governs the intermediate. Each is extraordinarily accurate within its domain. Their mutual incompatibility at boundaries — most acutely at the interface of quantum mechanics and gravity — remains one of the central unsolved problems in theoretical physics.

A second class of puzzles concerns unexplained numerical coincidences: why the fine structure constant α ≈ 1/137, why there are exactly three generations of fermions, why the Koide formula for lepton masses holds to 99.97% accuracy with no known mechanism. These are not derivable from the Standard Model; they are inputs to it.

We propose that both classes of problems may have a common origin: the absence of a framework that treats all levels of organisation as instances of the same recursive structure. The present paper introduces such a framework — the Resonant Hierarchy — not as a complete theory but as a conceptual hypothesis with concrete structural results and testable numerical predictions.

Scope of this paper. We distinguish sharply between two types of claim. Structural results follow from the logic of the model without free parameters. Numerical observations were found by exploration and are presented as patterns requiring independent verification. We do not conflate them.

The paper is organised as follows. Section 2 defines the recursive structure. Section 3 specifies the resonance function F. Sections 4 and 5 present structural results and numerical observations respectively. Section 6 describes all twelve levels. Section 7 lists open questions. Section 8 discusses the status of the framework.

§ 2

The Recursive Structure

At each level n of physical reality, we identify three elements:

Definition — Level Structure
Pₙ — objects at level n Cₙ — connections between objects at level n Eₙ — emergent properties of level-n objects
Examples: at level 2 (quarks), P₂ are quark fields, C₂ is the strong force (k·r), E₂ are colour charges (r, g, b). At level 4 (atoms), P₄ are nuclei and electrons, C₄ is electromagnetism, E₄ are shell structure and valence.

The transition between levels is governed by a single function:

The Recursion
Pₙ₊₁ = F(Pₙ, Cₙ, Eₙ)
F is the probability amplitude for a stable resonance to form at level n+1 from the objects, connections, and emergent properties at level n. |F|² is the probability of formation. Only configurations where this is maximal persist — equivalent to the principle of least action applied across levels.

The key structural claim concerns the origin of emergent properties. Rather than postulating E independently at each level, we propose:

Emergence Relation
Eₙ = ∂Cₙ₋₁ / ∂Pₙ
The emergent properties at level n are what the connections of the previous level distinguish about the current level's objects. Verified for levels 2–9: colour charge from direction vectors (level 2), mass and electric charge from confinement (level 3), Pauli exclusion from phase (level 4), temperature from vibrational amplitude (level 6), gravity from mass accumulation (level 7).
§ 3

The Resonance Function F

F decomposes into four factors, each enforcing a necessary condition for a stable resonance to form:

The Four Factors of F
F(Pₙ, Cₙ, Eₙ) = e^{−(Δf)²/2σ²} · frequency resonance · e^{−|∑D|²/2σ²} · directional closure · σ(C − C_min) · sufficient connectivity · e^{iΦ} · phase coherence
Frequency resonance: objects must vibrate at compatible frequencies.
Directional closure: the sum of direction vectors must approach zero — ∑D = 0. In 3D this forces a minimum of three components, predicting three quarks.
Sufficient connectivity: connections must exceed a threshold for the configuration to be stable.
Phase coherence: the phase factor e^{iΦ} implements Fermi–Dirac statistics. Two identical fermions with ΔΦ = 0 give F = 0 — the Pauli exclusion principle without postulate.
§ 4

Structural Results

The following results follow from the logic of the model. They do not involve free parameters or numerical fitting.

4.1 Three quarks from geometry

The directional closure condition ∑D = 0 requires that the sum of direction vectors of all component vibrations vanishes. In three-dimensional space, the minimum number of non-collinear vectors that can form a rigid closed configuration is three — a triangle. This is a mathematical fact, independent of any physical assumption. The proton therefore has exactly three quarks.

Result 4.1
∑D = 0 in ℝ³ → min(|{Pₙ}|) = 3
This is why there are three quarks in a proton and three colour charges. It follows from the dimensionality of space, not from SU(3) gauge theory (though the two descriptions are consistent).
4.2 Pauli exclusion from phase antisymmetry

For two objects in identical quantum states, all parameters coincide — including phase Φ. The phase factor in F then gives:

Result 4.2
ΔΦ = 0 → e^{iΔΦ} = 1, F_antisym = 0
The antisymmetrised amplitude vanishes identically for two identical fermions. The Pauli exclusion principle is not a separate postulate — it is a consequence of phase coherence in F.
4.3 Phase transitions of water

Temperature at level 6 is the mean amplitude of molecular vibrations. A phase transition occurs when thermal energy equals connection energy — the point where the resonance condition C = Cmin is exactly satisfied:

Result 4.3
T · ΔS = ΔH
Predicts boiling point of water at 373 K and freezing point at 273 K exactly, with no free parameters. The same condition applies to all substances.
4.4 Force carriers encode level properties

The quantum of Cn — the force carrier at level n — carries exactly the emergent properties En of that level. This follows from the recursion: gluons carry colour (E₂), photons carry charge information (E₃), W/Z bosons carry isospin (E₄), gravitons couple to energy-momentum (E₇). This is not separately postulated; it is a consequence of the structure.

§ 7

Open Questions

We list the principal open problems honestly. These are not weaknesses of the hypothesis but its frontier.

  • OQ·01
    The origin of the first vibration E₁ = (f, A, D, Φ) is the single free input of the entire framework. The recursion cannot explain where the first vibration comes from — this is the cosmological initial condition problem in a new form.
  • OQ·02
    Exact derivation of α = 1/137 Our approximation 1152/(π·e) ≈ 134.9 reaches 98.5%. The exact derivation of the fine structure constant from first principles remains open in this model and in all of modern physics.
  • OQ·03
    The Higgs scale Why the Higgs field has vacuum expectation value v = 246 GeV — the hierarchy problem — is not addressed by the framework in its current form.
  • OQ·04
    Dark matter particle identity The framework predicts dark matter as a resonance configuration with E₂ = 0, E₃(q) = 0, E₃(m) ≠ 0. The specific mass of such a particle does not emerge from the model.
  • OQ·05
    Exact generation masses The framework gives qualitative reasoning for three fermion generations via amplitude attenuation. Deriving the exact masses of the second and third generations from first principles remains open.
  • OQ·06
    Mathematical formalisation of F F is described conceptually. A rigorous Lagrangian formulation within quantum field theory — with renormalisability, Lorentz invariance, and exact equations of motion — is the essential next step for the framework to become a falsifiable physical theory.
§ 8

Discussion

The Resonant Hierarchy is a conceptual framework, not a complete theory. The distinction matters. A conceptual framework organises known phenomena into a coherent picture and suggests where to look for new structure. A complete theory makes precise quantitative predictions derivable from a well-defined mathematical formalism. We have the former; the latter requires substantially more work.

What the framework does offer is a unified perspective. The same three elements — objects, connections, emergent properties — appear at every scale. The same resonance condition governs every transition. Properties that are postulated independently in the Standard Model (colour charge, electric charge, spin, Pauli exclusion) emerge as structural consequences of the recursion.

The numerical observations in Section 5 are striking but must be treated with caution. Mathematical constants like π and e are sufficiently rich that many numerical coincidences can be constructed post hoc. The observations presented here are consistent with the framework — but consistency is not derivation. The test of a physical theory is prediction, not pattern matching.

The neutrino mass prediction (mν₁ ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻⁴ eV, from the seesaw relation interpreted as level-crossing resonance) is testable in principle by the KATRIN experiment. This is the clearest point of contact between the framework and ongoing experimental physics.

We offer this work as an invitation — to physicists who may find the recursive structure worth formalising, and to anyone who has asked why the universe is organised the way it is.
